Privacy Policy

Chronicles — last updated 18 August 2026

In short: Chronicles needs an account, so it stores your e-mail address and your reading progress on a server. It also measures how the app is used, which you can switch off. We sell nothing to anyone, and the stories themselves are read from the app, not from us.

Who we are

Chronicles is a reading app about historical figures and events, published for Android on Google Play. This policy explains what the app stores, where it goes, and what you can do about it. It replaces an earlier version which described Chronicles as an app that collected nothing; that stopped being true when accounts and synchronisation were added.

Data we collect

Your account

Chronicles requires an account, so that your reading progress follows you to a second device. When you sign up we store:

  • your e-mail address, used to sign in and to reach you about your account;
  • a username you choose, shown only to you;
  • your password, which is stored only as a cryptographic hash by our authentication provider. Nobody, including us, can read it back.

Your reading

So that your place in a story is the same on every device, the following is stored against your account on our server:

  • which chapters you have completed and which stories you have opened;
  • which characters you have unlocked, and when;
  • your reading streak and the date you last read something.

Your answers

Some chapters end with a quiz, a poll or a choice point. Quiz answers are not stored anywhere — a quiz is a self-test while you read, not a grade. Poll votes and choices are stored, one row per question, so the app can show how other readers answered. Other readers only ever see totals; nobody can look up how you voted.

Feedback you send

If you send us a bug report or an idea from inside the app, we store the message you typed together with the app version and the platform. Feedback goes nowhere else.

Usage statistics

Chronicles measures how the app is used, through Google Firebase Analytics. That covers:

  • which screens you open, and how long a session lasts;
  • a small, fixed set of actions: opening a story, finishing a chapter, finishing a story, unlocking a character, answering a quiz, voting in a poll, making a choice, reaching the daily reading limit, and opening or pressing the button on the subscription screen;
  • your device model, operating system version, app version and app language;
  • a pseudonymous identifier that Firebase assigns to this installation, linked to your account identifier so that one reader on two devices is counted once.

This includes an approximate location. Chronicles asks for no location permission and cannot read your GPS, but Firebase derives a country and region from the IP address of each request. That counts as approximate location, and it would be misleading to leave it out.

It never includes the text you write, your e-mail address or your password.

You can switch this off. Go to Profile → Settings → Privacy and turn off Usage statistics. From that moment nothing further is collected. It is on unless you turn it off.

Data stored only on your device

The following never leaves your phone, and disappears when you clear the app’s data or uninstall the app:

  • your theme (light, dark or system) and your language preference;
  • whether the daily reminder is on, and the time you picked for it;
  • your profile picture. If you choose a photo from your gallery, the app remembers where that file sits on your device — the picture itself is never uploaded;
  • your e-mail preferences (we send no e-mail yet);
  • how many stories you have opened today, for the free daily limit;
  • your choice about usage statistics;
  • a copy of your reading progress, so the app also works without a connection.

Who else sees it

We do not sell data and we do not share it for advertising. Two companies process data on our behalf, because the app could not work without them:

  • Supabase — the database and sign-in service holding your account, your reading progress, your poll and choice answers, and your feedback.
  • Google (Firebase Analytics) — the usage statistics described above, including the pseudonymous installation identifier and the approximate location derived from your IP address.
  • Google (Firebase Cloud Messaging) — delivery of the two kinds of notification we send from our servers, and only if you have switched them on. It receives the registration token for your installation and the text of the message.

Everything travels over an encrypted connection (HTTPS).

Your rights

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom you have the right to access, correct, export or erase your personal data, and to object to processing. In practice:

  • Switch off usage statistics — Settings → Privacy, at any time, effective immediately.
  • Delete your account — Settings → Delete account, after one confirmation. This is immediate and permanent: your account, your reading progress, your unlocked characters, your poll and choice answers and any feedback you sent are removed from our database in a single step, and the same data is cleared from the device you are holding. There is no backup to restore from and no way to undo it. You do not need to write to us and we do not keep a copy.
  • Ask for a copy of your data — Settings → Request my data, which opens an e-mail to us with the request already written. We answer within 30 days, normally much sooner. There is no automatic export button; the data is sent to the address you write from.
  • Anything else about this policy, at the address below.

How long we keep it: your account data stays for as long as the account exists. Delete the account and it is gone at that moment — we do not keep an archived copy and cannot recover it afterwards.

You can also complain to your national data protection authority. In the Netherlands that is the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.

Notifications

Every notification is optional, each kind can be switched on and off separately under Profile → Settings, and Android will not let us send any of them until you have granted permission. They come in two kinds, and the difference matters for your privacy.

Created by your device

The daily reading reminder, at a time you choose, and the warning that your reading streak is about to end. Both are scheduled entirely by your own phone. No push service is involved, no message is sent to or from a server, and we have no way of knowing whether they appeared or whether you opened them.

Sent by us

Two kinds are sent from our servers through Google’s Firebase Cloud Messaging: a reminder to come back if several days pass without reading, and an occasional suggestion for a story you have not opened yet. Both are off unless you turn them on.

To send them we need to store a registration token that identifies your installation of the app — not you, and not your phone number — together with your device model, your time zone and your app version. The time zone is what lets us send in your evening rather than ours. We also record which of these messages we sent you and whether you tapped it, so we can tell whether they are useful or merely annoying.

Turning both of these off unregisters your device again, and the token stops being used. We never put personal details in the text of a notification: it names a story, never your name, your email address or anything you have written.

Permissions

None of the permissions Chronicles requests give access to your files, your contacts or your location:

  • VIBRATE — the short haptic feedback when you tap a chapter or unlock a character. A normal permission; it asks for no confirmation.
  • POST_NOTIFICATIONS — needed to show the daily reminder described above. Android asks you for this one, and only after you have finished your first chapter. If you say no, the rest of the app is unaffected.
  • RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED and WAKE_LOCK — let a reminder you enabled survive a restart of your phone and arrive on time. Without them the reminder would silently stop after every reboot.
  • INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE — used to sign you in, synchronise your progress, load the polls and send the usage statistics described above.

The app also declares com.google.android.c2dm.permission.RECEIVE, the permission an app needs in order to receive push messages, because the Google libraries behind the reminder and the usage statistics declare it. Chronicles sends no push messages and is not registered to receive any: no push token is ever requested, and the reminder is scheduled by your own device’s alarm clock.

Offline use

All story text and all images are bundled inside the app, so you can read without a connection. The quizzes, polls and choice points come from our server and are simply absent when you are offline — the chapter reads exactly as it otherwise would. Progress you make offline stays on your device and is sent up the next time you have a connection.

Children

Chronicles is suitable for all ages, has no chat, and shows no content written by other readers. It is not directed at children under 13, and creating an account requires an e-mail address. We do not knowingly collect data from children under 13; if you believe a child has created an account, write to us and we will remove it.

Advertising and payment

This version of Chronicles shows no advertising and contains no working purchase. A subscription screen exists and describes a paid tier, but nothing can be bought yet, and the screen says so itself. If real advertising or a real purchase is added in a future version, this policy is updated before that version is published.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version is published on this page with a new date at the top. The version that applies to you is the one published here.
